# Photonics in Asia Pacific outside China: preliminary scoping

**Status at 16 November 2026. For consideration, not for decision.**

## What this is

Five days of desk research by AI, run in stages, on whether and where a private investor could invest in photonics. It is a scoping exercise. It is not a conclusive test of the thesis, because it did not reach most of the information such a test needs (see "What was not examined").

## The question and the mandate

- Where does demand for photonics outrun supply for long enough to matter, and which companies can be reached?
- Asia Pacific only, excluding mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau. No company with Chinese shareholders, manufacturing in China, or manufacturing with Chinese companies.
- Minority stakes with influence, with a cheque of up to US$500m. No unproven markets or products.
- Return target, holding period and fund location were not set. Results were measured against a reference of 2.0x and 15% a year.

## What the research supports

- **Demand is large and growing.** Optics for AI data centres is a market of roughly US$18–23bn in 2026. Two internal models put 2030 at US$42–56bn, mostly on assumed inputs.
- **Supply is short in specific steps:** indium-phosphide substrates, laser wafers and the best laser chips. Buyers have signed supply contracts of four to six years, the longest to 2031, and are prepaying for capacity.
- **Profit sits with the scarce steps.** Laser makers earn margins above 40% at present; module assemblers about 10%.
- **Listed companies are priced for this.** Minority stakes at November 2026 prices do not reach 2.0x in any central case.
- **The China rule removes most of the regional supply base.** No company has a verified pass.

## What is not established

- **How long the shortage lasts.** "Through 2028" is a judgement at low to medium confidence. Nothing in the record supports or rules out a shortage of 15–20 years.
- **True demand beyond 2030.** No sourced forecast was built.
- **Wafer and laser prices after 2027.** No public source gives a price path. This is the assumption the one lead depends on.
- **Any restriction on Chinese suppliers.** None covers optical modules today. A proposed rule would exclude plants in this region as well.

## What was not examined

- **Private companies.** Thirteen were profiled, all Japanese, from their websites. Korean and Taiwanese registries and industry lists could not be opened.
- **Most sources.** At most about a third were opened, and many of those only in part or as tool summaries.
- **Primary evidence.** No company, customer, adviser or industry expert was contacted. No paid market research or company database was used.
- **Whole areas:** sensing, medical, industrial and defence photonics; non-AI optics as an investment case; services and distribution.
- **Shareholder look-through** for any company.

## The one lead

LandMark Optoelectronics (Taiwan), a maker of laser wafers.

- Its stated target is 2.5 times output in 2027 and again in 2028, with signed orders above this year's revenue.
- On company evidence, a central case returns about 1.6x at NT$1,900 a share, and 2.0x if bought near NT$1,550.
- It has a stated plan to place up to 4.4% of its shares with strategic investors.
- Against it: 71% of sales go to Chinese customers; its China-rule pass is provisional; US$500m buys under 10% at the current price; the case fails if wafer prices fall about 5% a year.

A second wafer maker, VPEC, was examined and does not work at current prices.

## What a conclusive test would need

1. A company database covering private firms in Japan, Korea and Taiwan, with shareholders and accounts.
2. Direct conversations with companies, their customers and sector advisers, especially on prices after 2027.
3. A sourced demand and supply model by step, to 2035.
4. Agreed return target and holding period.

## For the reader to consider

- Is the opportunity strong enough to fund that fuller study?
- Should the China rule, the minority-only stance or the cheque size be revisited, given how much each narrowed the field?
- Should LandMark be tracked against the entry price above while that study is decided?

*Figures come from the research programme's own outputs and have not been independently verified. Share prices were moving up to 10% a day at the time of writing.*
