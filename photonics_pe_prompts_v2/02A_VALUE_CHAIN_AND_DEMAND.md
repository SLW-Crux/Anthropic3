# Stage 2A — Value chain, architectures and demand

**Required context:** `00_MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`; the approved Stage 1 handoff, Tier 1 ledger, critical claims register, gap register and research agenda. Start with the capability declaration.

## Mandate
Describe how the industry is structured and where demand realistically comes from. This stage is about **structure and demand only**. Segment profitability is Stage 2B; PE theses are Stage 3. Challenge growth narratives and adoption assumptions throughout.

Depth follows the Stage 1 research agenda: deep work on the segments and end markets it prioritised, a light scan of the rest. Say which is which.

## Research tasks
1. **Value-chain map.** Using the Stage 1 segment taxonomy: materials and substrates, epitaxy, wafers, lasers and light sources, design tools/IP, foundries, electronic-photonic integration, assembly and packaging, optical components, test/metrology, manufacturing equipment, modules and interconnect products, systems, distribution and value-added resale, specialist services. Cover IP and licensing models as well as physical products. For each: what is sold, to whom, who the representative suppliers are, and what it depends on.
2. **End-market scan.** For each end market in the master scope, give size indicators, growth drivers, cyclicality, customer structure and the supplier types that serve it. Communications will dominate the available evidence; give industrial, defence, medical and sensing markets a fair hearing, because established cash-generative photonics businesses often sit there.
3. **Communications architectures.** AI training and inference, scale-up versus scale-out, data-centre interconnect, telecom. Copper (DAC/AEC) versus pluggables, linear-drive optics, co-packaged optics (CPO), near-packaged optics (NPO) and optical I/O: where each is technically and economically competitive, by reach and data rate.
4. **Adoption bottlenecks.** Serviceability, reliability, heat, external light sources, power, yield, qualification, standards, ecosystem and manufacturing readiness. Compare announced timelines with what has actually shipped.
5. **Demand scenarios to 2030** (accelerated / base / delayed), with a view to 2035 only where defensible. These are **illustrative ranges, not forecasts**. Show unit drivers, attach rates, price trajectories and customer capex. Tag every input with its claim label and grade. Separate theoretical addressable market, serviceable demand and realistic supplier revenue, and remove double counting between components, modules and systems.
6. **Dependence on AI capital spending.** State how much of each scenario depends on hyperscaler and AI capex continuing, and what each segment looks like if that spending pauses for two years.
7. **Geography and policy.** Clusters, foundry and packaging dependencies, export controls, trade restrictions, supply concentration and government support. Analyse demand and technology globally, but map the **Asia Pacific ex-China supply base in depth** — which value-chain segments have meaningful independent companies in Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, South-East Asia, India and Australia, and which are dominated by US, European or Chinese players. Assess Chinese capacity and pricing as a competitive threat to each segment.
8. **Manufacturing-partner demand.** Which European and US photonics companies already manufacture, or have announced plans to manufacture, through Asia Pacific ex-China partners or sites, in which segments, under what arrangement (contract, licence, joint venture, own plant), and why? Which are moving production out of China? Identify the segments where Western design and Asian manufacturing are most separable, and those where they are not (for example where process know-how is the product). Grade this evidence like any other claim.
9. **Substitutes and architectural shifts** that could remove demand for a segment altogether.

## Required deliverables
A. **Value-chain map** (table): segment, products/services, representative suppliers, customers, dependencies, economic function.

B. **End-market table:** size indicator, growth, cyclicality, evidence grade, depth of work done (deep / light).

C. **Architecture and adoption assessment:** where each technology wins, bottlenecks, and realistic timing with confidence.

D. **Demand scenarios (file where possible):** inputs tagged sourced versus assumed, calculations visible, key sensitivities, AI-capex dependence.

E. **Geographic and policy risk map.**

F. **Gate self-assessment and Stage Handoff**, including the segments Stage 2B should analyse in depth.

## Gate pass criteria
- Every segment in the taxonomy appears in the value-chain map.
- Every in-scope end market has been scanned, with depth of work disclosed.
- Every scenario input is tagged; no forecaster figures are blended.
- Double counting is addressed explicitly.
- Each major adoption claim is paired with its strongest counter-evidence.
- The Asia Pacific ex-China supply base is mapped by segment, and Chinese competition is assessed.
- Evidence for and against manufacturing-partner demand is presented, with examples graded.
- No profitability ranking or PE thesis has been produced.

**Stop after Stage 2A.**
