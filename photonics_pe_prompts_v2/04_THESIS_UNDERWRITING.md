# Stage 4 — Investment thesis deep dives and financial underwriting

**Required context:** `00_MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`; the approved Stage 3 shortlist, handoff and scorecard; Stage 1–2B ledgers and files. Do not silently replace or redefine the approved theses. Start with the capability declaration.

**Recommended model:** Fable 5.2.

## Mandate
Determine whether each shortlisted thesis can generate compelling **risk-adjusted equity returns** from achievable operations, rather than from optimistic technology adoption or exit-multiple expansion.

The default investment is a **minority stake with influence** (see Ownership in the master instructions), not a buyout. Do not apply acquisition leverage; any debt is company-level borrowing the company itself would take on. Model a control deal only if the Stage 3 handoff asks for one.

These models describe an **archetype business**, not a real company. Their purpose is to show which assumptions the returns depend on, not to predict a return.

## Research tasks (same structure for every shortlisted thesis)
1. **Archetype definition.** Products, customers, qualification cycle, commercial maturity, competitive advantage, ownership structure and route to a minority stake, home country or countries within Asia Pacific ex-China, and investment size. Build two archetypes per thesis where the candidates differ in size: a significant minority stake in a small or mid-sized company, and a smaller percentage stake with negotiated rights in a larger one. Show what stake the cheque buys.
2. **Operating economics.** Revenue drivers, pricing, margins, customer and supplier concentration, cyclicality, utilisation, yield, capex (maintenance versus growth), working capital, R&D and cash conversion — anchored to the Stage 2B matrix.
3. **Comparables and precedents.** Listed comparables and **completed** transactions only, each with a source retrieved in this session and its date. Include completed minority investments by sponsors or strategics in each target country, with premiums or discounts paid and rights obtained. Record trading multiples with the retrieval date. Show regional precedents separately and explain how valuation, governance and shareholder practices differ by market (cross-shareholdings, founder or conglomerate control). Where no data exists, say so; do not estimate a multiple from memory.
4. **Route, rules and rights.** For each target country: routes to the stake (existing holder's block, new shares, partial tender, convertible or preferred instruments, co-investment with a founder); foreign-investment notification or approval thresholds, sector restrictions and large-shareholding disclosure rules, from opened official or legal sources; and the board seats, information rights, vetoes and exit rights a minority investor has realistically obtained.
5. **Illustrative model** over 5 and 7 years: volume, price, mix, EBITDA, capex, working capital, tax, cash flow, dividends and equity value. Deliver as XLSX or CSV with formulas visible, or as calculation tables showing each step. Every input is tagged **sourced** (with ID) or **assumption**.
6. **Investment structure.** Instrument, stake size, price relative to market, rights, and any company-level financing. All terms are assumptions; state the date and basis of any market reference used.
7. **Returns.** Base, upside and downside cases as gross MOIC and IRR, decomposed into earnings growth, cash returned and multiple change. Include a liquidity discount and the time needed to sell a large block. Show a base case with **no multiple expansion**.
8. **Break-even analysis.** State the conditions under which returns clear each reference hurdle (or the Fund Mandate hurdle if provided): maximum entry valuation, minimum margin, minimum growth, maximum exit discount. This matters more than the point estimates.
9. **Stress tests.** Delayed adoption, price erosion, overcapacity, capex overrun, loss of the largest customer, technology substitution, a two-year pause in AI capex, aggressive price competition from Chinese suppliers, currency movement against the fund's reporting currency, an uncooperative controlling shareholder, and a weak exit market.
10. **China screen in the model.** A minority holder cannot force a China exit. For a remediable archetype, model two cases: the company exits its China site as a negotiated condition, with cost and lost revenue as a tagged range; and it does not, in which case the investment does not qualify.
11. **Value-creation levers** with owner, KPI, cost, timing and expected effect. Separate what a minority investor can influence (capital allocation, overseas expansion, bolt-on acquisitions, governance, investor relations) from what management alone decides and what the market must deliver. Do not credit improvements that need control.
12. **Exit.** Block sale, sale to a strategic or sponsor, market sale, put or tag rights — supported by completed historical deals, not strategic logic alone. Assume Chinese buyers are unavailable, and say how much that narrows the buyer universe.
13. **If a manufacturing-partner thesis is shortlisted,** additionally model: customer concentration and loss of the largest Western partner; contract renewal and price-down clauses; who pays for capex and tooling; ramp and qualification delays; and the value of converting from pure contract work to licensed or co-owned products.
14. **If a new-build manufacturing thesis is shortlisted,** model it separately as a minority investment in a start-up operation: total capital to reach break-even and who provides it; build and qualification timeline; ramp, yield and utilisation; time to first revenue and to cash break-even; later funding rounds and dilution; the licence, supply or offtake agreement with the technology owner; minority protections and exit rights; and the outcome if the developer's product is delayed by two years or made elsewhere. State the loss if it fails. Tie each funding step to a solution-maturity milestone.
15. **Go/no-go tests**, diligence priorities and the information management would have to provide before a credible IC decision.

## Required deliverables
A. **Thesis papers** in identical structure: evidence, economics, obtainability of a meaningful minority stake, risks, exit.

B. **Models (files where possible)** with sourced inputs versus assumptions and a returns decomposition.

C. **Break-even and sensitivity comparison** identifying the two or three assumptions that dominate each thesis.

D. **Investment conditions, kill criteria (`K-…`) and diligence requests** per thesis.

E. **Relative underwriting attractiveness**, including where the evidence does not support a conclusion.

F. **Gate self-assessment and Stage Handoff** stating the archetypes and the valuation and ownership questions Stage 5 must test.

## Gate pass criteria
- Every model input is tagged sourced or assumption.
- Every comparable and precedent has a source retrieved in this session, or the absence of data is stated.
- A no-multiple-expansion case and break-even conditions are shown for each thesis.
- The AI-capex-pause stress test is run for each thesis.
- Returns are presented as conditional on stated assumptions, not as forecasts.
- No value creation is credited that would require control.
- No real company is named as available for investment.

**Stop after Stage 4.**
