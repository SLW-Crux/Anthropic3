# Stage 5 — Company target universe and executable PE strategy

**Required context:** `00_MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`; the approved Stage 4 handoff, thesis papers and models; the evidence, assumption and kill-criteria registers from earlier stages. Start with the capability declaration.

**Recommended model:** Sonnet 5.5.

## Mandate
Convert the surviving theses into a practical origination and acquisition programme built on **real companies**, without inventing ownership, financials, seller intentions or deal opportunities.

This stage carries the highest risk of plausible-sounding but wrong company facts. The rule is simple: **every statement about a company's ownership, financials, customers or transactions needs a dated source opened in this session; otherwise the field reads "Unknown".** A table with many honest "Unknown" entries is a good result. A complete-looking table filled from memory is a failure.

## Research tasks
0. **Regional research method.** Much of the best evidence is in local-language sources and national systems: for example EDINET and TSE filings in Japan, DART in Korea, MOPS in Taiwan, SGX and ACRA in Singapore, Bursa Malaysia, ASX and ASIC in Australia, BSE/NSE and MCA in India. Use them, say which you could and could not access, and note where translation limits confidence.
1. **Company universe** (`CO-…`) for each surviving thesis, **within Asia Pacific outside China only** (see the Fund Mandate geography rule): founder- and family-owned businesses, existing PE portfolio companies, corporate divisions, platform candidates and bolt-ons. Limit to roughly 15–25 companies per thesis, chosen for fit; note that the list is not exhaustive and how it was built.
1a. **For a manufacturing-partner thesis, build two lists.** (i) *Targets:* Asia Pacific ex-China manufacturers and sites, including Asian plants owned by European or US groups that could be carved out. (ii) *Partners:* European and US photonics companies that might place production with such a manufacturer — those with capacity constraints, China-based production they may want to move, or no volume manufacturing of their own. Partners are not acquisition targets. Record existing manufacturing relationships with sources; do not assert that any company is seeking a partner without evidence.
2. **Company record.** Name; ownership and source date; **China screen**, three tests each with source, date and result (none found / identified / Unknown): (i) Chinese shareholding, (ii) manufacturing sites in China, (iii) manufacturing joint ventures with, or outsourced production by, Chinese companies; plus non-excluding flags for China revenue dependence and Chinese material or component suppliers; headquarters and manufacturing sites; products and value-chain role; end markets and known customers; financial indicators (with the filing or registry they came from); commercial maturity; financing and transaction history; key risks; evidence gaps. Ownership changes quickly — give the date of the evidence, not just the fact.
3. **Three tiers, with evidence thresholds.** Companies that fail any China screen test are excluded and listed separately with the test failed and the source. Companies with an Unknown on any test may sit no higher than the monitor list. Report how many otherwise-suitable companies the screen removed, per thesis.
   - **Monitor list** — fits the archetype; nothing known about availability.
   - **Potentially approachable** — a structural reason an approach could be received (for example founder succession, non-core division, long sponsor hold), with the source.
   - **Credible acquisition candidate** — a documented signal: an announced sale process or strategic review, a sponsor hold beyond about five years, or a public statement by the owner. Without such a signal, a company cannot be in this tier.
4. **Origination design.** Industry clusters, advisers and sector bankers, corporate development teams, founder outreach, sponsor portfolios and specialist networks. Describe categories and how to reach them; do not invent named contacts.
5. **Platform versus single asset.** For a manufacturing-partner thesis, include how partnerships would be originated and structured (contract, licence, joint venture, carve-out with supply agreement) and what a sponsor adds in brokering them. Bolt-on fit, operating synergies, integration difficulty, concentration risk. Do not assume buy-and-build synergies are automatic; cite precedents where they worked or failed.
6. **Diligence plan:** commercial, technical, financial, legal/regulatory (including export control and foreign-investment review, by acquirer domicile), IP, operational, quality and ESG. Distinguish what public research can answer from what needs confidential data, management meetings, and site or customer work.
7. **Value-creation plans** for each platform archetype at 100 days, 24 months and 5 years, with KPIs, resources and capex, linked to the Stage 4 levers.
8. **Exit routes:** strategic and financial buyers, completed precedents, scale thresholds, downside outcomes.
9. **30/90/180-day origination and diligence programme**, prioritised by opportunity and uncertainty.

## Required deliverables
A. **Company universe (file where possible)** with source IDs, evidence dates and explicit "Unknown" fields.

B. **Tiered shortlist** with the evidence supporting each tier and how accessibility would be validated.

C. **Acquisition and portfolio-construction strategy:** deal sizes and structures by band, concentration limits.

D. **Diligence workplan** with key management, customer and technical questions.

E. **Value-creation programme, buyer universe and exit pathways.**

F. **30/90/180-day roadmap.**

G. **Gate self-assessment and Stage Handoff**, including a clear statement of which theses have real deal paths and which do not.

## Gate pass criteria
- Every ownership, financial and transaction field has a source ID and evidence date, or reads "Unknown".
- Every company in the top tier has a documented availability signal and a sourced China screen with "none found" on all three tests.
- No company outside Asia Pacific, or in mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau, appears as a target.
- The proportion of "Unknown" fields is reported per thesis.
- Each thesis is classified as having, or lacking, a real prospective deal path.
- No seller intention is asserted without a source.
- No venture-stage company appears as a target.
- No final IC recommendation has been made.

**Stop after Stage 5.**
