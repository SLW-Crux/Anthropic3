# Stage 3 — PE opportunity screening and competing thesis generation

**Required context:** `00_MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`; approved handoffs and files from Stages 1, 2A and 2B. Start with the capability declaration.

**Recommended model:** Fable 5.2.

## Mandate
Translate segment economics into **realistic and differentiated PE investment opportunities**. Separate buyout-suitable businesses from venture-stage, overly capital-intensive or structurally unattractive exposures.

## Research tasks
1. **Generate five to eight candidate theses** (`T-1…`) that are genuinely different from one another. Look across every level of participation — materials, IP and licensing, manufacturing, contract manufacturing, equipment, test, distribution and services — and include at least one thesis outside AI data-centre optics. Consider control buyouts, founder or family businesses, carve-outs, build-ups, adjacent established industrial platforms, and minority growth investments in established profitable companies only. **At least one candidate must be a manufacturing-partner thesis** (an Asia Pacific ex-China company manufacturing European or US photonics solutions; see the master instructions), in whichever form the Stage 2 evidence best supports; if several forms look distinct, treat them as separate candidates. It is screened and scored like the others and may be rejected. If fewer than five credible candidates exist, say so; do not pad the list.
2. **Pass/fail screen before scoring.** A thesis fails if: (a) target businesses are venture-stage — pre-revenue, pre-profit, or dependent on an unproven technology being adopted (this is PE, not VC); (b) no ownership route to control or meaningful influence exists; (c) no completed transaction of a comparable business can be found; (d) no plausible population of target companies exists in Asia Pacific outside China, able to pass the China screen (no Chinese shareholding, no manufacturing in China or with Chinese companies), under the Fund Mandate geography rule; or (e) it conflicts with another provided Fund Mandate parameter. For parameters that are NOT PROVIDED, note which deal-size band each thesis naturally falls into. For (d), consider how common China-based production is among companies of this archetype — if most have plants or manufacturing partners in China, the investable population may be very small — and name the countries and the kind of companies that make up the target population; precedent transactions under (c) may be global, but note whether any are in the region. Failed theses go to the rejected list with the reason.
3. **Thesis card** for every candidate: structural change; target archetype; why value accrues there (referencing Stage 2B); why PE can win; access and control route; moat; cash-generation profile; what PE can actually improve; exit options; timing; major risks; thesis-killing developments (`K-…`).
4. **Score surviving theses on a 1–5 scale** using the rubric below. Every score needs a one-line justification citing evidence IDs. A score without evidence defaults to 2 and is marked "unevidenced".

| Criterion | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | 15% | Declining or wholly dependent on one unproven adoption | Steady growth, some cyclicality | Growth supported in all three Stage 2A scenarios |
| Moat / value capture | 20% | Commodity, customers dictate price | Some lock-in, margins average | Qualification lock-in or IP with sustained above-average margins |
| Free cash flow | 15% | Cash-consuming, heavy capex | Positive but volatile | High, stable cash conversion |
| PE accessibility (in Asia Pacific ex-China) | 20% | No sellers, or strategics always outbid | Occasional deals, competitive | Many fragmented owners, repeatable deal flow |
| Value creation | 10% | Little PE can change | Standard commercial and operational levers | Clear, proven levers including bolt-ons |
| Exit | 10% | No precedent buyers | Some precedent | Several completed precedents and buyer types |
| Downside resilience | 10% | Fails if AI capex pauses | Hurt but survives | Diversified end markets, aftermarket revenue |

   Weights may be changed with stated justification.
5. **Keep evidence confidence separate from the attractiveness score.** Report both; never fold one into the other.
6. **Robustness.** Re-rank under two alternative weightings: one emphasising buyoutability and downside, one emphasising growth. State whether the ranking is stable. Because scores are judgements, treat differences of less than 0.3 as ties.

## Required deliverables
A. **Opportunity longlist**, including structurally attractive options that are not currently PE-investable.

B. **Screen results:** pass/fail per thesis with reasons.

C. **Scorecard (file where possible):** scores, justifications, evidence IDs, evidence confidence, three weightings.

D. **Thesis cards**, one consistent format.

E. **Shortlist of up to three** for deep underwriting, with the evidence each still needs. Fewer than three is acceptable.

F. **Rejected and deferred theses** with specific reasons and reopening triggers.

G. **Gate self-assessment and Stage Handoff** specifying the archetypes, economics and scenarios Stage 4 must underwrite.

## Gate pass criteria
- Every score has a justification and evidence ID, or is marked unevidenced.
- At least one candidate thesis lies outside AI data-centre optics, or its absence is explained.
- Evidence confidence is reported separately from score.
- Ranking stability is tested and ties are acknowledged.
- Rejected theses are recorded with reopening triggers.
- Every surviving thesis identifies its Asia Pacific ex-China target population.
- A manufacturing-partner thesis has been assessed, with its outcome and reasons stated.
- No specific company is recommended for acquisition.

**Stop after Stage 3** pending approval of the shortlist.
